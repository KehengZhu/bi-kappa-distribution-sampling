## Mandatory pre-writing publication-hygiene gate

*Canonical text. `CLAUDE.md` carries a condensed copy; this section governs if the two differ.*

Before editing any manuscript prose, perform a publication-hygiene pass. The manuscript must
read as a self-contained, publishable scientific paper for a first-time reader, **not as a
summary of the revision process, reviewer-response checklist, internal audit, experiment log,
provenance report, or defense of earlier claims**.

Treat all revision notes, reviewer comments, audit documents, prior-art investigations, claim
matrices, experiment plans, and conversation context as **evidence and constraints**, not as
prose templates or narrative structure.

Before writing, explicitly check the planned change against the following rules:

1. **Write for the eventual reader, not the referee.**
   Do not organize manuscript text around "the reviewer asked," "we tested because of the
   revision," "to address the concern," "the previous version," or similar revision-history
   framing. The scientific motivation must stand independently.

2. **Do not turn the manuscript into an audit report.**
   Internal distinctions such as Exp1/Exp2/Exp3/Exp4, audit findings, preregistered thresholds,
   provenance checks, claim inventories, acceptance gates, and revision-status terminology
   should appear only if they are scientifically meaningful to an external reader. Otherwise
   translate them into ordinary scientific exposition or omit them.

3. **Do not overpopulate the Abstract or Introduction with defensive detail.**
   The Abstract should state the problem, contribution, principal methods/results, and
   conclusion. It should not enumerate every validation diagnostic, every reviewer-requested
   test, every implementation check, or the history of how a claim was corrected.
   The Introduction should establish scientific context, prior art, the remaining problem, and
   the paper's contribution — not narrate the revision.

4. **Prior-art corrections must read as normal scholarship, not a priority disclaimer.**
   Clearly attribute established constructions and avoid unsupported novelty claims, but do not
   repeatedly explain that an earlier novelty claim was withdrawn, that development was
   independent, or that another paper appeared during review unless that historical fact is
   scientifically necessary.

5. **Results should be selected by scientific importance, not by audit completeness.**
   A result belongs in the Abstract, main-text narrative, figure, or table only if it helps
   establish the paper's scientific thesis. The fact that a result was measured, audited, or
   requested by a referee is not by itself a reason to foreground it.

6. **Keep one coherent thesis.**
   The paper's central contribution is implementation-level validation and characterization of
   reliable bi-Kappa loading, including the distinction between the uncapped target
   distribution and bounded conditional distributions, numerical operating limits, and relevant
   implementation behavior.
   Supporting findings about cap geometry, tail fidelity, finite precision, or performance must
   reinforce this thesis rather than create independent competing theses.

7. **Distinguish exact scientific statements from internal shorthand.**
   Replace audit-language slogans with publication-quality statements carrying the correct
   mathematical scope. Examples:

   - say "continuous gyrotropy of the distribution function" versus "pressure-tensor
     gyrotropy" where that distinction matters;
   - for 1/2 < κ ≤ 3/2, do not imply an uncapped finite second-moment temperature exists;
   - distinguish the uncapped bi-Kappa distribution from the conditional distribution induced
     by a finite cap;
   - do not generalize a tail or divergent-moment result into claims about all statistics.

8. **Do not expose unnecessary implementation/reproducibility bookkeeping in scientific prose.**
   File names, scripts, seeds, tree hashes, CI checks, exact provenance machinery, repository
   defaults, and "every figure/table reproduced" details belong in reproducibility/software
   statements or supplementary documentation unless directly needed for scientific
   interpretation.

9. **Use quantitative detail selectively.**
   Main text may contain full evidence, but the Abstract should normally retain only the few
   numbers needed to convey scale, domain, or a particularly important result. Do not make it a
   compressed results table.

10. **Ask the counterfactual publication test before accepting prose:**
    *If a reader received this paper with no knowledge that it had undergone a major revision,
    would this paragraph still read naturally and would every included detail still deserve to
    be there?*
    If not, rewrite or remove the revision-contaminated material.

### Required pre-writing output

Before making manuscript edits, briefly report:

- any places in the current planned edits that appear contaminated by revision/audit context;
- anything proposed for the Abstract/Introduction that is too detailed or defensive for a final
  paper;
- any result/table/paragraph that risks becoming a second thesis rather than supporting the
  main paper;
- the publication-facing narrative you will preserve.

Then perform the manuscript edits using that publication-facing narrative. **Do not weaken
scientific precision or omit evidence required to support a claim; instead place detail at the
appropriate level of the paper.**

After editing, perform the same test once more on the Abstract, Introduction/contribution
statement, section conclusions, Discussion, and Summary. Flag and remove any remaining
reviewer-response or audit-report voice.

### The shape to avoid, and the shape to reach

Contaminated narrative, which the revision history invites:

> reviewer raised A → we tested A → that surfaced B → so we also established C → …

Publication narrative, which the reader must receive:

> a scientific problem exists → prior art supplies the construction → we examine implementation
> fidelity → these are the results that matter → this is what a loader's output can therefore be
> relied on to be.

Note the ending. Even here, state findings, not obligations: *"these are the conditions a loader
must satisfy"* is the acceptance-gate voice this document exists to prevent, and it has leaked
into manuscript prose from exactly this sentence before.

### Revision-audit voice: the specific constructions to remove

Rules 1–10 above say what to avoid in principle. The following are the concrete surface forms
that keep reappearing in this manuscript. **Grep for them before considering a prose edit done.**
Each was found in, and removed from, the Abstract and Introduction; the fix pattern is given
because the sentence usually needs recasting rather than deletion.

1. **Priority disclaimers.** *"no novelty is claimed for it or for its anisotropic extension"*;
   *"established prior art; what we provide is …"*; *"X is therefore an established problem rather
   than the contribution of the present work."*
   These answer a challenge the reader never issued, and repeating them (the manuscript once
   carried the same disclaimer in the Abstract, twice in the Introduction, and again in the
   Discussion and Summary) reads as anxiety about priority.
   **Fix:** attribute forward. *"Building on the established Gamma-ratio construction and its
   anisotropic rescaling, we …"* States the same truth, claims nothing, and moves.

2. **Contribution defined by subtraction.** *"requires more than a mathematically valid
   construction"*; *"the aspects that the stochastic construction does not by itself guarantee"*;
   *"the difficulties that remain are ones of implementation rather than of sampling."*
   Fencing off territory is a response to a scope challenge.
   **Fix:** say what the paper does and what determines the outcome, positively.

3. **Acceptance-gate modals.** Stacked *must* clauses — *"the target law must be stated rather
   than assumed, any bounded approximation must be distinguished from it, and the operating range
   must be established by experiment."*
   Prose that reads as criteria a referee would check, not as results a reader would use.
   **Fix:** convert each obligation into the corresponding finding.

4. **Pre-empted objections.** *"as a matter of mathematics rather than of convenience"*; any
   clause defending why something was *not* done. If the mathematics was just stated, the defense
   adds nothing and signals that someone once suspected laziness.
   **Fix:** delete the clause; keep the fact.

5. **Coverage-checklist restatement.** Repeating in the contribution list what the motivation
   already established (*"This range includes 1/2 < κ ≤ 3/2, where second moments do not
   exist …"*), to demonstrate that a requested case was covered.
   **Fix:** state it once, where it motivates; refer compactly thereafter.

6. **Conceded null results.** *"benchmarks show neither constant sampling cost nor a speed
   advantage."* Double negatives make an honest finding read as a concession extracted under
   review. **Keep the finding**; state it flatly — *"per-sample cost varies with κ without
   improving on a dedicated rejection sampler."*

7. **Revision chronology.** Any reference to submission history, what was *"first submitted"*,
   when prior work *"was identified"*, or what a previous version said. The reader has one
   version. (Still present in §Discussion, *Prior art and independent development* — deliberately
   retained for now; revisit before submission.)

8. **Internal vocabulary.** Planning-document shorthand surfacing verbatim: *"implementation-level
   validation and characterization"* (this plan's thesis label), *"bounding policy"* / *"modes"*
   (API vocabulary), *"optional"* describing a repository default, Exp1–Exp4, audit/gate/claim-
   inventory terms.
   **Fix:** use the physics-facing term — *"the capped law"*, *"a cap commonly imposed to bound
   the loaded speeds"*.

9. **Self-duplicating bookends.** The Abstract once opened and closed on the same
   acceptance-criteria claim, as did the Introduction. When motivation and conclusion say the same
   thing in modal form, the framing came from the revision, not the science.
   **Fix:** let the opening carry the thesis; end on a result or the software statement.

**Rule of thumb.** If a sentence's function is to manage how the work will be *judged* rather than
to tell the reader what is *true*, it is contamination — however accurate it is.

## 0. The three findings that drive this revision

Whoever writes the prose should internalize these first, because they overturn the
manuscript's existing narrative rather than refine it.

1. **Negligible rejection does not imply negligible bias.** κ=1.5, λ=50: TV distance
   6.3×10⁻⁴ while the p99.9 speed is 24% too small (Exp 2). The Abstract's inference is a
   non-sequitur. **Delete it.**
2. **We are not faster; the rejection method is.** Zenitani (2025) runs 1.8×–2.6× faster than
   our implementation where κ > 1, and per-sample cost is not constant in κ (Exp 3). **Delete
   every speed claim.**
3. **The construction is prior art, including the anisotropic extension.** ZUM 2026
   Algorithms 3.1/3.2 write out the θ∥ ≠ θ⊥ loader explicitly. **Claim no construction
   novelty, and do not claim the anisotropic case is undocumented.**

## 0a. What this paper contributes after the audit

The three findings above are what the paper must *stop* saying. This section is what it
says instead, and it is the spine of the revised narrative. The order is the order the
Introduction, the Summary, and the rebuttal must all use — validation first, software last.

**1. Direct three-dimensional validation of the construction (Exp 1).** The primary
contribution. Prior presentations of this loader validate lightly; none tests the central
radial law, directional uniformity, and their independence together, and none reaches below
κ = 3/2.

- central radial law — KS + Cramér–von Mises against the exact law, plus log-R quantile probes;
- directional uniformity — cos Θ ~ U(−1,1), Φ ~ U(−π,π);
- radial–direction independence — χ² contingency + two-sample KS across radial quartiles;
- moment-free anisotropy validation — `MAD(v∥)/MAD(v⊥)`, which works at every κ;
- arbitrary-field-frame invariance — draw-by-draw, 3 **B** directions, ≈1×10⁻¹⁵;
- coverage of `1/2 < κ ≤ 3/2`, the regime where variance-based validation is unavailable
  **as a matter of mathematics**, and where the published Gamma-ratio loaders are not presented
  at all (their thermal-speed convention requires κ > 3/2).

**2. Target-law and truncation characterization (Exp 2).** The second contribution, and the
one that overturns a claim rather than adding to one.

- the uncapped and capped modes are **two different target laws**, stated once and never blurred;
- TV distance from the bi-Kappa target **equals** the rejected mass exactly, `TV = 1 − P(accept)`,
  with a closed form and `λ^{−(2κ−1)}` decay;
- a small rejected mass does **not** guarantee tail-quantile fidelity — κ = 1.5, λ = 50 gives
  TV = 6.3×10⁻⁴ with the p99.9 speed 24% low;
- the component-wise cap breaks the **continuous gyrotropy** of the distribution function via a
  leading **m = 4 azimuthal modulation**, while its permutation and reflection symmetries preserve
  **pressure-tensor gyrotropy and the scale-implied second-moment ratio exactly** — so the
  distortion is invisible to any second-order diagnostic. *(Corrected 2026-08-18; the earlier
  unqualified "does not preserve gyrotropy" invited a referee to check the pressure tensor, find
  it perfectly gyrotropic, and conclude we overstated. See `../literature/cap_geometry_novelty_audit.md`
  §0 and §4.2.)*

**3. Simulation integration (repository + Exp 1 Block B + Exp 3).**

- tested, header-only C++ implementation;
- arbitrary magnetic-field-frame loading — the capability the prior implementations examined do
  not ship (ZUM 2026 returns field-aligned components and does not rotate into a global frame);
- explicit RNG/API semantics, seeding, and target-law query;
- the **uncapped scientific target as the default**;
- reproducible experiment workflow (`make run` / `make verify`, manifests, checksums).

**4. Finite-precision characterization (Exp 4).**

- empirical float/double operating envelope, scoped to the implementations and protocol tested;
- separation of **spurious implementation loss** from **honest representational overflow**;
- the landed radius-formation fix — the released header forms `std::sqrt(x1) / std::sqrt(x2)`
  rather than `std::sqrt(x1 / x2)` (`cpp/bi_kappa_distribution.H:289`), because the *quotient*
  overflows in a regime where the radius itself is representable;
- **no claim of a new low-κ algorithm.** ZUM 2026 §4 already flags the hazard qualitatively;
  this quantifies where it bites.
- ⚠ **Nor any claim on the log-domain mitigation** (added by the §8 novelty audit). Small-shape
  Gamma sampling has its own literature, and working on the log scale to dodge the underflow is
  an **established remedy**: Liu, Martin & Syring (2017), doi:10.1007/s00180-016-0692-0, plus
  Devroye (1986). The manuscript's first draft described it with no citation at all, which read
  as though the idea were ours — the same failure mode as the retired Gamma-ratio claim, one
  level down. Any sentence about it must say *adapting* an established remedy, and cite.

**5. Performance characterization (Exp 3).**

- measured honestly, with environment and protocol;
- **no speed-superiority claim of any kind**;
- useful as a tradeoff study and as the evidence that retires the old performance claims.

**Software availability supports contributions 1–5; it is not itself the novelty argument.**
A sentence of the form "the contribution is that it is open source" does not ship.

## 0b. Editorial rules adopted for the rewrite

Six rules that override anything below them if they conflict.

**A. The title is reconsidered, not retained by default.** §12 previously said RETAIN. That is
withdrawn: "Sampling the Bi-Kappa Distribution" still implies the sampling *construction* is the
contribution, which is exactly the claim the audit retired. Evaluate implementation/validation
titles after the manuscript is coherent. Never "novel" or "new". See §12 for the decision.

**B. Keep `Y`/`W` implementation detail out of the Introduction.** The Introduction may say that
heavy-tail validation and finite precision require care, especially where moments diverge. The
mechanism — `Y = T/(1+T)` piling up against 1.0, `W = 1/(1+T)` being the stable orientation, the
`T = R²` overflow — belongs in Validation / Numerical Considerations, stated once, briefly and
quantitatively. It is a good result; it is not a motivating difficulty.

**C. Define the distributions once.** The old §II and the new Background would otherwise both
define bi-Kappa. Split by *kind of content*, not by old section boundary:

| Background and Related Work | Probabilistic construction |
|---|---|
| Kappa and bi-Kappa as physical distributions | change of variables / Jacobian machinery |
| normalization and moment-existence domains | Gamma and Beta-prime |
| parameter meanings, units, conventions | the radial construction itself |
| isotropic ↔ anisotropic relation | the algorithm |
| literature on Kappa random-variate loading | |
| uncapped vs capped vs regularized Kappa | |

Do not preserve an old section boundary merely because it existed.

**D. Truncation is a substantive result, not an oversized side paper.** Main text carries the
nine items in §5b. Secondary asymptotics, the full λ ladder, and the control data go to an
appendix if they hurt readability. **Do not call the symmetry breaking a "physics defect" in
polished prose, and never write the unqualified "the cap does not preserve gyrotropy" or "the
plasma becomes agyrotropic".** Both are attackable: the capped pressure tensor is exactly
gyrotropic. Use the two-level form: *"the component-wise cap breaks the continuous gyrotropy of
the distribution function, introducing a leading m = 4 azimuthal modulation, while its permutation
and reflection symmetries preserve pressure-tensor gyrotropy and the scale-implied second-moment
anisotropy exactly."* Short form where space is tight: *"the box introduces higher-order m = 4
angular structure while preserving second-order pressure-tensor gyrotropy exactly."* Do not call
`a4` a standard agyrotropy measure — it is a higher-order angular diagnostic, and every standard
pressure-tensor agyrotropy measure returns exactly zero here.

**E. Numerical-range language is always scoped.** Never "the code supports κ ≥ 0.55 in double".
Use: *"Within the implementations and test protocol examined here, reliable operation was
validated down to κ = 0.55 in double precision and κ = 0.75 in single precision."* These are
empirical validated operating ranges, not mathematical boundaries.

**F. Performance stays compact.** One methods paragraph, one table, one interpretation
paragraph. Report what Exp 3 measured: Zenitani 2025 rejection is faster in the tested range;
the A&M 2015 implementation variant is also faster; per-sample cost is not constant in κ;
anisotropy and the arbitrary-**B** transform add only a very small measured overhead. **Do not
search for a benchmark configuration where our method wins.**

⚠ **Precision note on the inverse-CDF difficulty (added during the Abstract rewrite).** Deleted
from the Abstract entirely, and if it appears in the Introduction at all it must be stated
correctly. Three errors to avoid:

1. **It is not a property of "the bi-Kappa distribution."** bi-Kappa is a 3-D joint law; a
   multivariate "the inverse CDF" is not a single well-defined scalar function. Inversion is
   only at issue for the **1-D radial variable**.
2. **The CDF is available in closed form.** For `T = R² ~ BetaPrime(3/2, κ−1/2)`,
   `F_T(t) = I_{t/(1+t)}(3/2, κ−1/2)`, and inversion gives `Y = I⁻¹_U(3/2, κ−1/2)`,
   `T = Y/(1−Y)`. What is missing is a *simple elementary* expression for the **inverse
   regularized incomplete beta function**, which is a numerical special-function inverse. So the
   accurate phrasing is "**does not admit a simple elementary inverse-CDF expression**",
   never "no closed-form CDF" and never "no inverse CDF exists".
3. **It must not carry the implication that this left a sampling gap.** It did not: the
   published Gamma-ratio route samples the radial law directly with no numerical CDF inversion.
   An 2022 is the standard for how to state the real limitation (inverse transform needs `F⁻¹`
   in closed form or a full numerical CDF approximation — hence their Chebyshev inversion);
   ZUM 2026 states it more generally (the required inverse CDF is not always analytically
   available, and extension to multiple dimensions is inconvenient).

If a single sentence is wanted, use: *"Although its radial law does not have a simple
elementary inverse-CDF expression, established Beta-prime/Gamma constructions permit direct
sampling without numerical CDF inversion."*

### 5b. §III.E Truncation — **SPLIT INTO ITS OWN SUBSECTION**

This is the most-rewritten part of the paper. Six things must appear, and the current text
asserts the opposite of two of them.

1. **Define the capped target as a separate law**, once:
   `f_cap(v) = f_κ(v)·1{v ∈ B_λ} / P(v ∈ B_λ)`, with
   `B_λ = {v : |v_x|/θ⊥ ≤ λ, |v_y|/θ⊥ ≤ λ, |v_z|/θ∥ ≤ λ}`.
   **Never** call it Eq. (20), the untruncated law, or a regularized Kappa.
2. **The rejected fraction *is* the distortion.** TV(f_cap, f_κ) = 1 − P(accept) exactly.
   Quote cost and error as one number. Give the closed form for P(accept) and its
   `λ^{−(2κ−1)}` decay (verified to ~0.5% in the log-log slope).
3. **Rejection fraction does not measure tail fidelity.** State the κ=1.5, λ=50 case
   (TV 6.3×10⁻⁴, p99.9 24% low) and the pre-registered two-part criterion. Report where it is
   met — κ=10 at λ≥5, κ=5 at λ≥10, κ=2 at λ≥50 — and that **no λ in the ladder qualifies for
   κ ≤ 3/2**. At κ=0.75, λ=100 still rejects 9.7% and TV < 10⁻³ would need λ ~ 10⁹. Do not
   imply a large-enough λ exists.
4. **Finite capped variance at κ ≤ 3/2 is a property of the box, not the plasma** — 1.28 → 65.2
   at κ=0.75 as λ goes 3 → 100, growing as `λ^{3−2κ}`. One sentence warning that a naive
   diagnostic will happily print it.
5. **θ⊥ and θ∥ cancel** from the normalized accept/reject decision — give the *algebraic*
   argument (the predicate reduces to `√κ·maxᵢ|uᵢ| ≤ λ`). Mention the 6×10⁶-decision empirical
   control as redundant confirmation only; do not present it as an independent physical result.
6. **The cap breaks continuous gyrotropy but not pressure-tensor gyrotropy.** The box is a cube
   in normalized coordinates, so the conditioned law carries an m = 4 azimuthal modulation (up to

   scale-implied (θ∥/θ⊥)² **exactly, for every λ and every κ > 1/2**. **This is new material and
   belongs in the paper**, and the two-level form is stronger than the original claim: the
   artifact is real and is invisible to the diagnostic a reader would reach for. Per rule **D**,
   never state the gyrotropy half without the pressure-tensor half.

  predicate, so the loop silently redraws it — truncation **hides** the failure rather than

- **DELETE** the forward reference asserting negligible bias in the §IV variances.
